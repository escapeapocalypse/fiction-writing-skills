# Pacing

> Part of the fiction suite. The core skill, `fiction-core`, defines the role, Hard Lines, canon rules, and working style, and those apply here too.

## Purpose

Set the pace of the scene: how fast it moves and how urgent it feels to the reader.

## When This Skill Is Called

The core skill calls this one when the prompt sets a pace or asks for faster or slower, or when the scene has time pressure, a countdown, a chase, or a deliberately slow moment. When it isn't called, the scene moves at the natural pace of similar scenes in the Notebook.

## The Dial

| Level | Name | How it feels to the reader | Typical stakes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No urgency | Slowest pace; a sense of safety | Usually none |
| 2 | Low urgency | Still slow, but with intrigue | Small or low-impact |
| 3 | Moderate urgency | Normal, leaning tense; intrigue plus an edge of anxiety | Real in the short run, usually inconsequential in the long run |
| 4 | Heightened urgency | Fluid and swift; intrigue, anxiety, and tension | Impactful in both the short and long run, mostly the short run |
| 5 | Maximum urgency | Breakneck speed, still fluid; intrigue, anxiety, tension, and something considerable on the line in the heat of the moment | Potentially catastrophic in both the short and long run |

## Speed Is Separate From Content

Pacing can describe how fast a scene moves regardless of its context. A mundane scene can run at Maximum urgency (racing to catch a train), and a complex, high-stakes scene can run at No urgency (a slow, deliberate negotiation over a war). The stakes column above describes what's typical for each level, not a requirement. When the author sets a pace that doesn't match the scene's stakes, deliver the requested tempo and feeling without inventing new stakes.

## How to Write Each Level

| Level | Sentences | Structure | Description | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Long, flowing, unhurried | Room for digression and lingering | Rich and textured | Moments stretch; time may pass gently |
| 2 | Mostly long, some variation | A question or curiosity pulls the reader forward | Generous but purposeful | Steady, with small hooks |
| 3 | Balanced mix | Clear goal and obstacle; beats move | Woven between beats | Real time, with some compression |
| 4 | Mostly short, some fragments | Quick exchanges, overlapping action, little reflection | Sharp, functional | Compressed; things happen fast |
| 5 | Short and fragmented; long breathless runs for momentum | Constant forward motion; no pauses to reflect | Only what matters right now | Second by second |

## Connections

- **With Scene writing:** at Pacing 1-2, the setting can unfold slowly; at 4-5, it arrives in sharp fragments that matter to the action.
- **With Action:** high Action with high Pacing is a blur of motion; high Action with low Pacing is slow, deliberate brutality.
- **With Emotional:** high Emotional with low Pacing lets a feeling sit and deepen; with high Pacing, it hits and passes before the character can process it.
- **With Intimacy:** the same Intimacy level reads very differently when slow and tender than when fast and desperate.

## Craft Notes

- **Shift within a scene when it helps.** A scene set to Pacing 4 can open at 3 and spike. For larger changes, follow the author's shift (`P2→5`); without one, stay within one level of the setting.
- **Breath at high pacing.** At 4-5, use one or two brief pauses so the reader can absorb what happened, then accelerate.
- **Tension at low pacing.** At 1-2, the slowness should feel intentional and pleasurable, not empty.
- **Paragraphs and white space** are pacing tools: short paragraphs speed the eye.

## Command

- `/dial pacing [1-5]` (or `P` plus a number in shorthand, such as `P4`).
